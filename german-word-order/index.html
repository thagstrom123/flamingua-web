German Word Order: The V2 Rule

German word order confuses everyone at first. In English, we say "I go tomorrow to Berlin." In German, that's wrong. The verb has to be in second position: "Ich gehe morgen nach Berlin." Or if you start with time: "Morgen gehe ich nach Berlin."

This is the V2 rule (verb-second rule), and it's one of the first grammar concepts that trips people up. Once you understand it, German sentences start making sense.

The V2 Rule: Verb in Second Position

In main clauses, the conjugated verb must be in the second position.

Not the second word — the second position. A position can be one word or a phrase.

Position 1 Position 2 (VERB) Position 3+
Ich gehe morgen nach Berlin.
Morgen gehe ich nach Berlin.
Nach Berlin gehe ich morgen.

Notice: No matter what you put first, the verb stays in second position. If the subject isn't first, it comes right after the verb.

Common Sentence Patterns

Pattern 1: Subject First (Most Common)

Subject → Verb → Everything Else

  • Ich wohne in Berlin. (I live in Berlin.)
  • Er trinkt Kaffee. (He drinks coffee.)
  • Wir lernen Deutsch. (We learn German.)

This is just like English. Easy.

Pattern 2: Time/Place/Other First (V2 Kicks In)

Time/Place → Verb → Subject → Rest

  • Morgen gehe ich nach Berlin. (Tomorrow I go to Berlin.)
  • Heute arbeite ich nicht. (Today I'm not working.)
  • In Berlin wohne ich seit einem Jahr. (I've lived in Berlin for a year.)

The subject (ich) moves to position 3 because the verb must stay in position 2.

Pattern 3: Questions (Verb First)

Verb → Subject → Rest

  • Wohnst du in Berlin? (Do you live in Berlin?)
  • Trinkst du Kaffee? (Do you drink coffee?)
  • Lernst du Deutsch? (Do you learn German?)

In yes/no questions, the verb moves to first position.

When the Verb Goes to the End

In subordinate clauses (after words like weil, dass, wenn, ob), the verb goes to the end of the clause.

  • Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Berlin wohnen will. (I'm learning German because I want to live in Berlin.)
  • Ich denke, dass er Kaffee trinkt. (I think that he drinks coffee.)

At A1, you don't need to master this yet. Just know that weil (because) and dass (that) change word order. You'll practice this more at A2.

Common Mistakes

  • Keeping English word order: "Ich morgen gehe nach Berlin" is wrong. It's "Ich gehe morgen nach Berlin" or "Morgen gehe ich nach Berlin."
  • Forgetting V2 when you start with time/place: "Morgen ich gehe" is wrong. It's "Morgen gehe ich."
  • Putting the subject before the verb in questions: "Du wohnst in Berlin?" sounds weird. It's "Wohnst du in Berlin?"

How to Practice

  • Build sentences with different starts: Take "Ich gehe nach Berlin." Say it starting with time: "Morgen gehe ich nach Berlin." Starting with place: "Nach Berlin gehe ich morgen." Practice the inversion.
  • Read German out loud: When you see German text, notice where the verb is. Count the positions. This trains your brain to expect the V2 pattern.
  • Speak full sentences: Don't just drill grammar rules. Use them: "Heute gehe ich zur Arbeit." "Morgen lerne ich Deutsch." Say it until it feels automatic.
