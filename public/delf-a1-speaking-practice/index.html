DELF A1 Speaking Practice (Production Orale)

The oral test is the most stressful part of DELF A1. You sit face-to-face with an examiner and need to speak French clearly, naturally, and confidently. Most apps teach you phrases but don't give you real speaking practice.

What is the DELF A1 Production Orale?

The DELF A1 speaking test (production orale) lasts 5-7 minutes and has three parts:

  • Entretien dirigé (Interview): Answer questions about yourself (name, age, family, hobbies, work)
  • Échange d'informations (Information exchange): Ask and answer questions using prompt cards
  • Dialogue simulé (Role-play): Act out a simple scenario (shopping, asking for directions, making an appointment)
The challenge: You can't fake speaking ability. You either can speak or you can't. Reading phrases in an app doesn't prepare you for the real conversation.

Common Speaking Mistakes

What causes students to fail the oral test:

  • Poor pronunciation: The examiner can't understand you
  • Long pauses: Taking too long to form sentences
  • Memorized phrases: Sounding robotic instead of natural
  • Panic: Freezing when asked unexpected questions
  • No practice: First time speaking out loud is during the exam

Traditional Speaking Practice Methods

Alliance Française classes:

  • €1,800-2,800 for full A1 course
  • Group format — you speak maybe 10 minutes per 2-hour class
  • Fixed schedule

Online tutors:

  • €25-50 per hour (iTalki, Preply, Verbling)
  • Great for feedback, but expensive for daily practice
  • Need to book sessions ahead

Language exchange apps:

  • Free, but inconsistent quality
  • Partners often don't correct your mistakes
  • Hard to practice exam-specific scenarios
