Der, Die, Das — German Articles Explained

Every German noun has a grammatical gender — masculine, feminine, or neuter. The article that goes with it (der, die, or das) is one of the first things you need to learn. This is the foundation of German grammar at A1.

The Definite Articles (the)

In English there's only "the." In German, it depends on the noun's gender:

GenderDefinite ArticleExample
Masculinederder Mann (the man)
Femininediedie Frau (the woman)
Neuterdasdas Kind (the child)
Plural (all)diedie Kinder (the children)

The Indefinite Articles (a/an)

The indefinite article also changes by gender:

GenderIndefinite ArticleExample
Masculineeinein Tisch (a table)
Feminineeineeine Lampe (a lamp)
Neutereinein Buch (a book)

Notice: Masculine and neuter share the same indefinite article (ein). The difference only shows in the definite article (der vs. das).

Example Sentences

  • Der Kaffee ist heiß. — The coffee is hot. (masculine)
  • Die Suppe schmeckt gut. — The soup tastes good. (feminine)
  • Das Brot ist frisch. — The bread is fresh. (neuter)
  • Ich möchte einen Tee. — I'd like a tea. (masculine, accusative)
  • Hast du eine Frage? — Do you have a question? (feminine)
  • Das ist ein Stuhl. — That is a chair. (masculine)

Tips for Remembering Gender

German gender often feels random, but there are patterns:

  • Usually masculine (der): days, months, seasons — der Montag, der Januar, der Sommer
  • Usually feminine (die): nouns ending in -e, -ung, -keit, -heit — die Lampe, die Wohnung
  • Usually neuter (das): nouns ending in -chen, -lein — das Mädchen, das Brötchen

The best strategy: Always learn the article together with the noun. Don't memorize "Tisch" — memorize "der Tisch." This habit pays off enormously as your German grows.

Common Mistakes

  • Guessing based on meaning: "Das Mädchen" (the girl) is neuter, not feminine — because of the -chen ending.
  • Forgetting plural uses "die": All plural nouns use "die" regardless of their singular gender.
  • Mixing up ein/eine: "Ein" is for masculine and neuter. "Eine" is only for feminine.
