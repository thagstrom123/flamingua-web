Goethe A1 Study Plan: 8-Week Guide

You have 8 weeks to prepare for the Goethe A1 exam. That's enough time if you're consistent and focus on the right things.

This study plan assumes you're starting with some basic German knowledge (at least a few weeks of study). If you're starting from zero, add 4-6 weeks of foundation work before this plan.

Quick Exam Overview

The Goethe-Zertifikat A1 has four parts:

  • Reading (Lesen): 25 min — Short texts, emails, signs
  • Listening (Hören): 20 min — Announcements and conversations
  • Writing (Schreiben): 20 min — Fill out forms, write short messages
  • Speaking (Sprechen): 15 min — Introduce yourself, ask/answer questions

Most people struggle with speaking. I took the Goethe A2 in Winterthur and noticed this — people who were fine with reading and listening would freeze during the speaking part. That's the section you need to practice most.

8-Week Study Plan

Week 1-2: Foundation & Grammar

Goal: Cover all core A1 grammar topics

Focus areas:

  • Present tense verb conjugation (sein, haben, regular verbs)
  • Articles (der, die, das) and noun gender
  • Accusative case basics (den vs der)
  • Word order (V2 rule)
  • Question words (wer, was, wo, wann, wie)

Daily routine: 30-45 min grammar study + 15 min speaking out loud

Week 3-4: Vocabulary & Speaking Practice

Goal: Build A1 vocabulary (500-600 words) and practice self-introduction

Focus areas:

  • Core vocabulary (family, food, work, hobbies, time, numbers)
  • Practice Selbstvorstellung (self-introduction) until automatic
  • Common questions and answers for Part 1 of speaking test
  • Daily speaking practice: describe your day in German

Daily routine: 30 min vocabulary + 30 min speaking practice

Tip: Record yourself speaking. Listen back. You'll catch mistakes you don't notice while speaking.

Week 5-6: Exam Format Practice

Goal: Understand exam format and time limits

Focus areas:

  • Do 2-3 full practice tests (find them on Goethe-Institut website)
  • Time yourself on each section
  • Practice writing short messages (30-40 words) in 20 minutes
  • Practice speaking prompts with a timer (15 min total)

Daily routine: 1 section per day (reading, listening, writing, or speaking)

Common mistake: People spend too much time perfecting grammar and skip speaking practice. The speaking section is 15 minutes and worth 25% of your grade. Practice it daily.

Week 7-8: Final Review & Speaking Drills

Goal: Lock in speaking confidence, review weak areas

Focus areas:

  • Do 1 full practice test per week
  • Review mistakes from practice tests
  • Speaking drills: practice Part 2 (asking questions) and Part 3 (responding to requests)
  • Final week: focus 80% on speaking, 20% on review

Daily routine: 20 min review + 40 min speaking practice

Speaking Section Breakdown

This is where most people lose points. Here's what to expect:

Part What You Do Preparation
Part 1: Selbstvorstellung Introduce yourself (name, age, where you're from, job, hobbies) Practice this until you can say it perfectly without thinking. This is free points.
Part 2: Ask questions Ask your partner questions based on cards (work, hobbies, food, etc.) Practice W-questions (Wo...? Was...? Wann...?). Keep it simple.
Part 3: Respond to requests Your partner asks you for something (Can you help me? Can you bring bread?) Practice "Ja, kein Problem" and "Nein, tut mir leid." Don't overthink it.

What to Do the Week Before

  • Stop learning new things. Review what you know.
  • Practice speaking 30 min/day. Selbstvorstellung + random question prompts.
  • Do one final full practice test. Time yourself. Identify weak spots.
  • Get sleep. Don't cram the night before. Your brain needs rest to perform.

Day of the Exam

  • Arrive 15 minutes early
  • Bring your ID and confirmation email
  • The speaking test is with a partner (another test-taker). Don't worry if they're nervous — focus on your own performance.
  • If you don't understand a question, it's okay to ask "Können Sie das wiederholen?" (Can you repeat that?)

From my experience: Being very academic helped me with exams. But I saw most people struggle with the speaking part — which is ironically the most important for the C permit. If you can nail the speaking section, you're in good shape.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Spending too much time on grammar perfection: A1 doesn't require perfect grammar. You need functional communication.
  • Skipping speaking practice: You can't learn speaking by reading. You have to actually speak out loud.
  • Not timing yourself: The exam has strict time limits. Practice under timed conditions.
  • Panicking during the speaking test: If you make a mistake, keep going. Don't apologize or stop. The examiner is looking for communication, not perfection.
